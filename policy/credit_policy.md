# Sample Bank — MSME Manufacturing Credit Policy (Cash-Flow Based Lending)

> Illustrative sample policy written for the UdyamPulse prototype. It is not the policy of any real bank.
> Version 2.3 · Owner: Credit Policy Committee · Applies to: MSME Business Banking

## §1 Scope and definitions

### §1.1 Scope
This policy governs working-capital and term facilities to micro and small manufacturing enterprises
assessed primarily on consented operating data (GST, e-way bills, Account Aggregator bank statements,
electricity consumption and ERP data) rather than on collateral alone.

### §1.2 MSME classification
Enterprises are classified as micro or small using their Udyam registration under the thresholds notified by
the Ministry of MSME. The Udyam registration number must be verified before sanction.

### §1.3 Role of technology service providers
Analytics platforms (including UdyamPulse) act as technology service providers to the Bank. They produce
scores, memos and alerts as recommendations only. The Bank retains ownership of the credit decision,
disbursal, Key Fact Statement, credit-bureau reporting and grievance redressal.

## §2 Eligibility

### §2.1 Vintage
The enterprise should have at least three years of operations. Units with one to three years of vintage may be
considered only in the Approve band with a reduced limit under §4.2.

### §2.2 GST and statutory compliance
The enterprise must hold an active GST registration and must have filed GSTR-3B for at least eleven of the last
twelve months. Two or more consecutive late filings in the last six months require a credit officer's written comment.

### §2.3 Consent-based data
A valid Account Aggregator consent artefact covering bank statements for at least twelve months and a GST data
consent must be active at the time of assessment. Assessment must stop if consent is revoked.

### §2.4 Exclusions
The Bank does not lend to enterprises on the RBI wilful-defaulter list, to units with an existing NPA with any
lender, or for speculative or prohibited activities.

## §3 Assessment

### §3.1 Cash-flow based assessment
The primary assessment is the enterprise's operating cash flow and production evidence. Order book and dispatch
(e-way bills), bank inflows, electricity consumption and receivable quality are weighted ahead of collateral.

### §3.2 Factory Health Score bands
The Factory Health Score (300–900) is mapped to bands: 650–900 Approve, 550–649 Watch, 300–549 High risk.
Approve-band proposals may be sanctioned by a credit officer. Watch-band proposals require a mitigant plan and a
reduced limit. High-risk proposals should be declined with reasons and an improvement plan offered.

### §3.3 Explainability
Every score must be accompanied by its top reasons in plain language and a pillar-level breakdown. A credit
officer must not rely on a score whose reasons are unavailable.

### §3.4 Data reconciliation
GST turnover must be reconciled with bank credits. A mismatch above 25% must be explained by the borrower before
sanction (for example, multiple bank accounts or cash sales) and is a monitoring trigger under §8.2.

## §4 Limit setting

### §4.1 Working-capital limit
The fund-based working-capital limit is assessed at up to 20% of the annual GST turnover of the last twelve
months (turnover method), subject to the requested amount.

### §4.2 Band adjustment
The assessed limit is multiplied by a band factor: 100% for the Approve band and 60% for the Watch band.
No limit is assessed for the High-risk band.

### §4.3 Buyer-concentration adjustment
Where the largest buyer accounts for more than 50% of sales, the assessed limit is reduced by 15% and receivables
from that buyer should be routed through TReDS or an escrow arrangement.

### §4.4 Rounding and caps
Limits are rounded down to the nearest ₹1 lakh. Exposure to a single MSME borrower under this programme is
capped at ₹10 crore.

## §5 Pricing

### §5.1 Benchmark
All loans are linked to the Bank's External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR). The spread is fixed at sanction and
reset only on a credit event.

### §5.2 Spread by score
| Score | Spread over EBLR |
|---|---|
| 750–900 | 2.00% – 2.50% |
| 700–749 | 2.50% – 3.00% |
| 650–699 | 3.00% – 3.50% |
| 550–649 | 3.75% – 4.50% (with mitigants) |

### §5.3 Guarantee cover
Eligible loans should be covered under the Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE);
the guarantee fee may be passed through to the borrower as disclosed in the Key Fact Statement.

## §6 Security

### §6.1 Collateral-free lending
Loans to micro and small enterprises up to ₹10 lakh must not require collateral security. Larger loans may be
sanctioned without collateral where covered under §5.3.

### §6.2 Primary security
Hypothecation of stock and book debts. Stock and receivable statements may be drawn directly from the
borrower's ERP/Tally with consent instead of manual statements.

## §7 Covenants

### §7.1 Continuous data consent
The borrower shall keep Account Aggregator and GST data consent active for the tenor of the facility for
monitoring purposes. Revocation is the borrower's right; on revocation the Bank shall fall back to manual
periodic statements.

### §7.2 Statutory filings
GSTR-3B shall be filed by the due date every month. Two consecutive late filings trigger a review.

### §7.3 Receivables
Receivables from any buyer exceeding 45 days should be discounted through TReDS where available. The borrower
shall notify the Bank of the loss of any buyer contributing more than 25% of sales.

### §7.4 Banking discipline
Cheque or NACH returns for insufficient funds must not exceed one in any month. The borrower shall route at least
70% of business credits through the Bank.

### §7.5 Information
Quarterly stock and receivable statements (ERP-generated) and annual financial statements within 180 days of
year-end.

## §8 Monitoring and early warning

### §8.1 Continuous monitoring
All accounts are monitored using consented operating data. Early-warning signals include falling e-way bills or
electricity consumption, bank-balance stress, cheque/NACH bounces, buyer concentration, GST-to-bank mismatch and
late filings.

### §8.2 Special Mention Accounts
Accounts are classified as SMA-0 (1–30 days overdue), SMA-1 (31–60 days) and SMA-2 (61–90 days) in line with RBI
norms. Early-warning alerts should be acted upon before an account reaches SMA-1.

### §8.3 Response timelines
High-severity alerts: relationship manager contact within 1 working day and a unit visit within 3 working days,
followed by a limit review. Medium-severity alerts: contact within 3 working days. Low-severity alerts: review at
the next monthly call.

### §8.4 Revival and restructuring
For units showing incipient stress, the Bank shall consider corrective action under the RBI framework for revival
and rehabilitation of MSMEs, including restructuring, before classification as non-performing.

## §9 Decision authority and human oversight

### §9.1 Sanctioning authority
Only an authorised credit officer may approve or reject a proposal. Every decision must carry a written comment.

### §9.2 Use of AI
AI-generated scores, memos and alerts are recommendations. The borrower must be told that AI-assisted analysis
was used. No proposal may be rejected solely by an automated process.

### §9.3 Reasons and review
Rejected applicants must receive the principal reasons and may request a human review of the decision.

## §10 Digital lending and data protection

### §10.1 Digital lending
Disbursal shall be made directly into the borrower's bank account. A Key Fact Statement with the all-in cost
(APR) must be provided before execution. Only data necessary for the credit purpose may be collected;
biometric data shall not be stored.

### §10.2 Consent and retention
Data is processed only for the purpose stated in the consent notice. On revocation, further data pulls must stop
and previously pulled data not required by law must be deleted. Data shall not be retained beyond the period
required for the stated purpose or by regulation.

### §10.3 Data localisation
All borrower data shall be stored and processed in India.
